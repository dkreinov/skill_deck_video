Non-Human Research Workflow Autonomy
Causal Link to Self-Acceleration
Workflow autonomy measures the elimination of human execution latency from the system-development loop [cite: 11]. When agents can independently execute multi-step experimental pipelines over extended time horizons without human checkpoint validation, the iteration cycle shifts from human work-weeks to continuous machine execution [cite: 11].
Current Measurable Evidence
During the pilot evaluations conducted in early 2026, leading frontier developers documented extensive integrations of automated systems [cite: 12]. Google reported using machine assistance for pre-training data preparation, building evaluation frameworks, writing core infrastructure code, and designing optimization algorithms [cite: 12]. OpenAI documented embedding code-executing agents across all internal R&D workflows, deploying models as monitors and grading systems in pre-training and security evaluation pipelines [cite: 12]. Anthropic reported that the focus of its research staff has transitioned from writing code to orchestrating, directing, and reviewing autonomous agent outputs [cite: 12].
On the standardized Time Horizon 1.1 evaluation suite, public models achieved a 50% success threshold on tasks requiring an autonomous execution horizon of approximately 12 hours, while internal frontier configurations reached a threshold of 16 to 20 hours [cite: 12]. At the 80% task-completion threshold, public models demonstrated a 1.5-hour capability, whereas internal models achieved 3 to 4 hours [cite: 12]. On direct-token mathematical evaluation tasks without intermediate processing steps, public models maintained continuous operations for 4 minutes, whereas internal models lasted under 10 minutes [cite: 12]. On the Subversion Strategy Evaluation, public models achieved a 72% success score, while internal models scored up to 80% before encountering safety-related refusals [cite: 12]. While task-completion duration has historically doubled every four months since 2024, developer audits explicitly report that overall research and development velocity has not yet achieved a 2x compounding acceleration [cite: 12, 13, 14].

Automated Experimentation and Pipeline Output
Causal Link to Self-Acceleration
This metric serves as a quantitative multiplier of exploratory capacity [cite: 17]. When models are deployed to manage parallel hyperparameter sweeps, write custom CUDA kernels, and debug training runs, a single human researcher can manage hundreds of concurrent experiments, accelerating the discovery of novel optimization methods [cite: 11, 18].
Current Measurable Evidence
Technical audits of OpenAI's GPT-5.6 Sol model demonstrate extensive self-improvement capacity, with the model scoring 16.2 points higher on internal R&D evaluation suites than its predecessor, GPT-5.5 [cite: 19]. During deployment, Sol was provided with an underspecified prompt through the Codex platform, directing it to configure hyperparameter sweeps, select optimal GPU topologies, execute active training scripts, and verify downstream performance parameters [cite: 19]. Active deployment of this model resulted in average daily token output per researcher more than doubling its previous historical peak, alongside a sharp rise in concurrent pull requests and training iterations [cite: 19]. At Anthropic, engineers utilizing automated coding systems have achieved an eight-fold increase in daily merged code output compared to 2024 baselines [cite: 13, 20, 21].

Raw token volume and merged lines of code do not scale linearly with technological progress [cite: 20]. Highly automated code-generation pipelines often produce redundant test wrappers, verbose scripts, or context-blind configurations, potentially increasing downstream maintenance overhead and masking a flattening curve of actual architectural breakthroughs [cite: 20, 22].

Wall-Clock Interval Reduction in Generational Rotations
Causal Link to Self-Acceleration
Tracks the temporal compression of the design-train-evaluate cycle [cite: 8]. A shortened interval indicates that software-driven automation is successfully compensating for physical compute bottlenecks, driving rapid architectural turnover [cite: 7].
Current Measurable Evidence
Pre-training pipeline studies of open-science projects (such as OLMo 3) demonstrate that exploratory trials, failed pre-training runs, and ablation sweeps consume 82.2% of the total computational budget, establishing a primary wall-clock bottleneck [cite: 28]. Conversely, parameter-efficient adaptations (such as Low-Rank Adaptation updating only 1-2% of model parameters) consume only 12.5% (one-eighth) of the wall-clock time of full training runs [cite: 29].
Under practical training constraints, massively parallel environments (such as PPO collecting experience across 2,048 parallel simulations) reach competitive performance boundaries significantly faster in wall-clock time than sequential, sample-efficient frameworks [cite: 30]. Weather foundation models highlight this speed gap: GraphCast was trained in approximately 4 weeks on 32 TPU v4 chips [cite: 31], Pangu-Weather completed training in 16 days on 192 V100 GPUs [cite: 31], and the transaction model PRAGMA-L scaled to 32 H100s over a two-week period [cite: 29].

Energetic Requirements per Unit of Cognitive Capability
Causal Link to Self-Acceleration
Self-acceleration must overcome thermodynamic limits [cite: 33]. As absolute cluster sizes collide with regional power grid capacities, models must achieve rapid energetic efficiency gains—such as sparse parameter routing—to compress the watt-hours required per cognitive task [cite: 33, 43].
Current Measurable Evidence
In 2025, AI datacenter electricity consumption grew to represent 4.1% of global power grids [cite: 43]. While a human biological system consumes approximately 30 million kcal over a lifetime (~35,000 kWh) [cite: 33], training a single frontier model requires 100M kWh, representing a 2,857x energetic premium over biological processing [cite: 33]. To manage these constraints, sparse Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) architectures have compressed active-parameter ratios down to 6% of total weights [cite: 43], and distillation techniques have enabled a 412x reduction in model size (from 540B PaLM to 1.3B Helix-nano) while maintaining equivalent capabilities on knowledge benchmarks [cite: 43].
Data Availability and Publisher
Compiled from hardware technical specifications, utility integration reports, and hyperscale datacenter filings [cite: 33, 43, 44].
Confounders
Total energy footprint expansion masks local efficiency gains [cite: 16]. Developers may design highly efficient inference models but offset these gains by scaling up parallel test-time computation paths, increasing total energy consumption [cite: 16, 45].
Explicit Falsification Condition
Falsified if the average energetic cost (in watt-hours) required to resolve a standard, multi-step problem on advanced logic benchmarks fails to decline by at least 30% year-over-year.
